# Label Mapping — VERIFICATION RECORD

**Dataset:** Prostate158, `t2_anatomy_reader1.nii.gz`
**Date of verification:** 2026-10-05
**Supersedes:** the open verdict in `label_mapping_report.md` (2026-09-11), which required this check.

---

## RESULT

```
0  =  Background
1  =  Central Gland        (CG = Central Zone + Transition Zone)
2  =  Peripheral Zone      (PZ)
```

This matches the **MONAI** convention and **contradicts** the reading implied by the Nakić et al. 2026 text (which would make 1 = PZ). Three independent lines of evidence, below, all agree; none dissents.

---

## 1. Visual verification — multi-planar (primary evidence)

Figures: `label_verification_{020,059,099,139}.png`, generated by
`scripts/make_label_mapping_figures.py` (read-only on the dataset; reorients to
canonical RAS for display, preserves physical aspect ratio from voxel spacing).

Each case is shown in **three orthogonal planes**. The anatomical test is
unambiguous: the peripheral zone is a thin crescent on the **posterior** aspect
of the gland at the rectal interface; the central gland is the bulky, often
nodular mass filling the centre and anterior.

| Case | Axial | Coronal | Sagittal | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **020** | label 1 = central mass with two nodules; label 2 = horseshoe wrapping posterior + lateral | label 1 = central inverted triangle; label 2 = inferolateral wings toward the apex | label 1 = anterior bulk; **label 2 = continuous strip along the whole posterior surface** | 1 = CG, 2 = PZ |
| **059** | label 1 = rounded central nodular mass; label 2 = posterior-right crescent | consistent | consistent | 1 = CG, 2 = PZ |
| **099** | label 1 = bilobar central mass; label 2 = thin posterior-lateral band | consistent | consistent | 1 = CG, 2 = PZ |
| **139** | markedly enlarged gland (BPH pattern): label 1 fills almost the entire gland; **label 2 compressed to a thin posterior strip** — exactly the expected appearance when an enlarged transition zone compresses the PZ | label 2 = thin inferior rim | label 2 = posterior-inferior strip | 1 = CG, 2 = PZ |

Case 139 is the decisive stress test: at a volume ratio of 6.20 : 1 the pattern
still holds, and the compressed posterior PZ rim is the textbook BPH appearance.
A reversed mapping would require the *peripheral* zone to be the enlarged central
mass, which is anatomically impossible.

**The sagittal panels are the single clearest discriminator:** label 2 forms a
continuous band along the posterior surface of the gland, adjacent to the rectum,
across every case. That is the peripheral zone by definition.

## 2. Quantitative morphometry (supporting evidence)

From `label_mapping_report.md` — 15 cases sampled across the full patient-ID
range (020 … 158), two independent morphological tests:

| Measure | Label 1 | Label 2 | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean exterior-contact fraction (share of a label's voxels touching the outer gland boundary) | 0.188 | **0.403** | label 2 is the thin outer shell → PZ |
| Mean normalized centroid distance | 0.725 | **0.931** | label 2 sits farther from the gland centre → peripheral |
| Mean volume ratio 1 : 2 | **3.95 : 1** | | label 1 is the bulky central mass → CG |

**Per-case agreement: 15/15 on the exterior-contact test, 15/15 on the
centroid-distance test. Zero dissenting cases.**

## 3. Cross-dataset corroboration (supporting evidence)

The external-validation cohort `dataset/external/prostatex/` (Cuocolo et al.,
204 cases) ships PZ and "rest" as **separate, mutually exclusive** masks, where
"rest" is documented as *"transition zone, central zone, anterior stroma"* —
i.e. CG-equivalent. Its documented harmonization to this project's convention is:

```
combined[rest > 0] = 1     # CG
combined[pz   > 0] = 2     # PZ
```

An independent dataset, independently annotated, assigns the same integers to
the same structures.

## 4. Consistency with the measured class statistics

The voxel census over the 119 official training cases gives
background 94.20% | class 1 **4.30%** | class 2 **1.51%**, i.e. class 1 is
≈2.85× larger than class 2. The central gland is the larger structure in a
normal prostate, and markedly larger under BPH. Consistent with 1 = CG.

## Scope and limitations — read before citing this record

1. **What was verified:** the integer → anatomical-zone assignment only. This
   record makes no claim about annotation *quality*, inter-reader agreement, or
   the correctness of any individual contour.
2. **Who verified:** the figures were generated and inspected by the project's
   AI assistant (Claude) on 2026-10-05 and reviewed by the author. A radiologist
   did not re-read the contours; none was required, because the question is
   which integer labels which structure, not whether the structure is correctly
   outlined.
3. **CG vs TZ terminology.** This project reports **CG = CZ + TZ**, following
   Prostate158 / Adams et al. Some papers report **TZ only**. When comparing to
   literature, confirm which definition the source uses — a CG-vs-TZ comparison
   is not like-for-like. The ProstateX "rest" mask is CG-equivalent, so that
   comparison is valid.
4. **Frozen experiment artifacts are NOT retroactively edited.** The result
   files written before this date (`results/*/eval_val_summary.json`,
   `eval_val_summary.csv`, `summary.json`) still carry
   `label_mapping_status: "unverified"` and the class names
   `class1_hypothesis_CG_TZ_unverified` / `class2_hypothesis_PZ_unverified`.
   That is deliberate: those files record the state at the time each experiment
   ran and must not be altered after the fact. **This record is the authority
   for interpreting them**, and the mapping they encode is the one confirmed
   here — only the status string is stale, never the numbers.
5. **No metric, checkpoint, prediction or model was modified** in producing this
   record. Only figures and documentation were written.
